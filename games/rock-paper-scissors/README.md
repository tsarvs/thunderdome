# Rock Paper Scissors

_The arena floor is scorched from a thousand prior bouts, and the floodlights haven't dimmed
since this show started running. Two combat chassis roll to their marks as the crowd loses its
mind. Neither one has a face, but both have exactly three attack forms hard-coded into their
combat firmware, and exactly one instant to commit to one of them. The ref-drone counts down.
Somewhere, a klaxon sounds. Then — impact._

A best-of-many combat exchange between two battle chassis, decided one simultaneous strike at a
time.

## Objective

Win more individual exchanges than your opponent across the whole bout. Whoever's landed the most
exchanges once the last strike has resolved takes the win light.

## How an exchange works

Every exchange is a single, simultaneous strike — no telegraphing, no reading your opponent's
servos, just three hard-coded attack forms and one commitment:

1. Both chassis privately lock in an attack form: **rock** (the crush plate slams down), **paper**
   (the wrap-net deploys), or **scissors** (the shear blades spin up). Whatever the housing looks
   like, every combat chassis in the league runs exactly the same three forms.
2. Both attack forms release at the exact same instant — neither chassis's targeting computer
   ever sees the other's choice before its own is already locked and committed. The league's
   anti-tamper rules make sure of it.
3. The exchange is judged by the arena's oldest, simplest ruleset:
   - Rock smashes scissors.
   - Scissors shears paper.
   - Paper smothers rock.
   - Matching forms cancel out — no exchange lands, and both chassis reset for the next one.
4. The arena resets instantly, and the next exchange begins. No damage carries forward except
   whatever pattern each chassis's own targeting computer chooses to log.

The bout runs a fixed number of exchanges (300 by default) — it's never called early just because
one chassis has a mathematically insurmountable lead. The league learned generations ago that
cutting a bout short the moment it looks decided just gets the result overturned in appeals.
Running every exchange keeps the scoreboard honest, and gives both combat computers the same,
known window to find a pattern in the other's firmware.

## Winning the bout

Once the last exchange has been judged, whichever chassis landed more exchanges wins the bout
outright. If both chassis landed the exact same number — including if every single exchange
cancelled out — the bout itself ends in a draw, and the win light stays dark for both.

## Good to know

- There's no armor, no energy reserve, no hit points here — every exchange is decided purely by
  the attack form committed in that instant. A sharp targeting computer still logs its opponent's
  past patterns and looks for a tell in the firmware, the same way any good fighter studies tape.
- If a chassis's targeting computer fails to lock in a legal attack form when the buzzer sounds,
  the league's rulebook is unforgiving in one of two configurable ways: either that single
  exchange is scored as a loss, or the whole bout is forfeited outright — the event organizer
  sets which rule applies before the first exchange ever fires.
